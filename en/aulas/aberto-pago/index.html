Lesson 7 · 16 min

Open vs. paid tools: what you can build for free and when to pay

A paid SEO tool sells data: search volume, competitor SERPs, backlinks, AI monitoring. For your own site, almost everything you need day to day comes from free sources: Search Console, Bing Webmaster, the server log, a local crawler, and the open Common Crawl graph. This lesson teaches you to tell the two apart and to decide when to pay.

02 · What each path delivers

Click each step. Prices read from the vendors' pages on 10/19/2026: check the site before you decide.

1. The free core

Performance, official AI citation, technical audit, AI bots in the log, and approximate backlinks all come from free sources. For keywords, autocomplete, Google Trends (relative index), and the real queries from Search Console.

That is enough for the work in lessons 2 to 6.

2. open-seo

open-seo is an open alternative (MIT) with an interface, an MCP server, and skills for agents. But the data comes from DataForSEO: the README says you need a key from them, and the hosted service charges 28% more per request.

It works as a reference for how to package SEO for an agent; it is not a data source of its own.

3. Firecrawl and Apify

Firecrawl turns pages into text for an LLM (map, crawl, scrape, search): free plan with 1,000 credits per month, Hobby at US$ 16 on the annual plan. Apify runs ready-made collection bots: free with US$ 5 in credit, Starter at US$ 19 per month.

A third-party bot that scrapes Google violates Google's terms of service, and the risk falls on whoever runs the bot.

4. Open tools for collection

For your own site: crawl4ai (Apache-2.0), Scrapy, and Crawlee crawl and convert to text. Scrapling (BSD-3) gets around anti-bot protections; use it only on your own site or with permission.

INEMA's open tools for your site are in the inemaseo-sistemas repository, taught in course 7.

03 · Your table of functions and the decision to pay

Copy it into a project file and fill it in. The agent can build the draft; you check the price on the site.

ferramentas.md: function, source, limit
| function | free source | honest limit | paid (if you need it) |
|---|---|---|---|
| site performance | Search Console (weekly export) | your site only; 16 months | — |
| AI citation (official) | Bing Webmaster: AI Performance (CSV) | no clicks; no API | paid AI monitor |
| technical audit | auditoria.sh / local crawler | no JS at scale | Screaming Frog |
| AI bots | server log | check the IP, user-agent can be spoofed | — |
| backlinks | Common Crawl (domain) + Search Console (Links) | no page or anchor; delay | DataForSEO / Ahrefs |
| keyword | autocomplete, Trends, Search Console | no exact volume | DataForSEO / Semrush |
| competitor SERP | — | scraping Google violates the terms | DataForSEO / Apify |
decisao-pago.md (template)
# Decision: <paid tool>
Date: <YYYY-MM-DD>
Decision that the data unlocks: <e.g.: choose between 3 topics with similar volume>
Free options already tried and the limit that blocked it: <...>
Price checked on the site on <date>: <amount and plan>
Key: in .env (outside the repo), used only for <purpose>
Reassess on: <date>
Result: <did you pay? was it worth it? what changed>
Simulation: illustrative example

Situation: a coffee shop with three topics for the month and no queries for them in Search Console yet.

  • Free tried: autocomplete and Trends show similar relative interest in all three.
  • Limit: without exact volume, you can't break the tie.
  • Decision: a one-off volume query on a paid provider, with the key in the .env and authorization for that purpose; reassess the following month.
  • To "see if it shows up in AIs": the official citation is in Bing AI Performance, for free.
  • To audit your own site: a local crawler does the job.
  • To "rank fast": no tool delivers a ranking position.
